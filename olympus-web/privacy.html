Privacy

Olympus has no server, no account and no telemetry. This page explains what that means, where the app’s own data lives, and the three cases where something does leave your machine.

2. What the app collects

Nothing. The application has no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, no usage counters, no update pings and no licence activation call. It does not phone home, and it works with the network switched off.

This is a design commitment, not a setting. There is no code path in the app that sends your projects, prompts, code, file paths, machine name, IP address or usage data anywhere.

3. Where the app’s own data lives

On your machine, in your operating system’s normal application-data location:

  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/Olympus
  • Windows: %APPDATA%\Olympus
  • Linux: ~/.config/olympus

That directory holds the project list, application preferences, window state, your licence key, and local logs. It does not hold your source code: your projects are referenced by path and are never copied into it.

Deleting the app does not delete this directory. That is deliberate — reinstalling should not mean rebuilding your project list — but it does mean the data outlives the install. Remove it by hand when you want it gone. Log files may contain fragments of terminal output, including paths and error text from commands you approved.

  • The transaction reference and amount, from our payment processor.

  • Anything you have chosen to send us in a support email, which is kept as long as it takes to resolve the matter and no longer.

5. When something does leave your machine

Three cases, and only three. In all of them, the data goes to a server you chose, not to us.

  • Your model server. Prompts and whatever the agent reads are sent to whichever OpenAI-compatible server is configured — normally Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM or llama.cpp on the same machine, which is why nothing leaves at all in the intended setup.
  • A cloud provider you configured inside opencode. If a provider is set up there, that traffic goes to that provider. The Content-Security-Policy that constrains the Olympus window does not apply to it, because the agent, not the window, makes the request.
  • A dev server, when you start one. Running a project sends the usual requests to its own localhost port. That is between you and your own project.

opencode is a separate third-party project with its own behaviour and its own privacy practices. We do not control it and we do not speak for it.

6. What “local-first” does and does not mean here

It means: no Olympus server, no account, no telemetry, no cloud component, no data held about your work, and the ability to use the entire application with no network connection.

It does not mean: that every part of the stack is local — opencode and your model provider are separate decisions; that traffic is encrypted end to end — traffic to a local model server is ordinary HTTP over loopback; that the agent is safe to leave unattended — it is a program with your permissions, and approval is a human process; or that we can make promises about opencode’s handling of your data.

7. This website

These pages are static files. They load three typefaces from Google Fonts, which means your browser makes a request to a Google server to fetch them and Google sees your IP address as a result. That is the only third-party request any page on this site makes, and it is about a font, not about you.

There is no analytics script, no tag manager, no advertising pixel, no social widget, no embedded video and no cookie on this site. script.js contains a dispatcher that emits an olympus:analytics CustomEvent for a handful of interactions and writes them to the browser console. It has no listener and no destination; it is a hook for a self-hosted tool if one is ever wanted, and it is a no-op as shipped. The pages also work fully with scripting disabled, in which case nothing at all is requested beyond the HTML, the CSS and the font.

Because the data we hold is a purchase record, your rights are mostly about access and correction. Email support@example.com to ask what we hold about you, to correct it, or to have it deleted. We will answer, and we will do it without asking you to prove anything beyond that you are the person who bought it.

10. Changes

This policy may change, in a later version published on this page with its own effective date. Because the app collects nothing and this site sets no cookies, a change here cannot retroactively affect data already gathered from your use of the software.

If you find a claim on this page that the software does not honour, that is a bug with legal weight. Report it at security@example.com or support@example.com and it will be treated as the first thing to fix.
