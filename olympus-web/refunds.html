The small print

Refunds

Fourteen days. No questions, no form, no retention script. This is the shortest page on the site because there is not much to say.

Home / Refunds

The promise

Ask within 14 days, get your money back

One condition: you bought it. Nothing else is required, and we will not ask you to justify it.

What you get

  • All of it back. The full $49, not a pro-rated remainder, not a credit.
  • No reason needed. You will not be asked what went wrong.
  • No form. One email is the entire process.
  • Back to the original method. Card, bank transfer, whatever you paid with.
  • Your licence ends and you stop using the software. We do not mind, and we do not follow up to find out why.

How to ask

Email support@example.com from the address you bought with, put “refund” in the subject, and say which purchase. That is the whole thing.

  • We acknowledge within three working days.
  • The refund is issued within five working days of that.
  • It then takes your bank as long as it takes, which is outside our control and is the only part of this you may have to wait for.

What the 14 days are for

Mostly for the opencode compatibility risk. Olympus depends on somebody else’s fast-moving project, and 0.1.0 is tested against opencode 1.18.x. A fortnight is enough time to open a real project, run a real task and find out whether the thing works for you. If it does not, that is a bad outcome for you and a full refund is the correct response to it.

It is also enough time to notice that the app is not something you want, which is a perfectly good reason on its own.

What is not refundable

  • After 14 days. Past the window we cannot refund a purchase. We would rather say that plainly than leave it implied.
  • The free build. It was never paid for, so there is nothing to return.
  • Third-party costs. Your opencode licence, your model server, your provider’s tokens and your electricity are not part of this purchase and cannot be refunded by us.

What happens after 14 days

Two things, and it is better to know about both before you buy.

  • If the software is broken. Email support@example.com. A defect that makes the software unusable is not a matter of taste and we will treat it as one. We cannot commit in advance to how many of these there will be, and we would rather not write a promise we cannot keep.
  • If we are not maintaining it. Your licence is perpetual: the build keeps running, the changelog stays published, and the version of opencode it worked with stays installable. A licence that stops working because the maintainer lost interest is a worse product than one that is never sold, and it is not what this is.

Where refunds are not the right answer

If you want a different operating system build, or a licence for a colleague, or a major-version upgrade when one exists, that is a new purchase rather than a refund — see pricing, and email if the situation does not fit any of the cases on it.

Volume purchases of two or more licences are refundable on the same 14-day terms, all of them together or none of them. Splitting a purchase to keep some seats and return others is not a thing we will do.

Statutory rights

Nothing on this page limits any right you have under the consumer law that applies to you, and where such a right gives you more than 14 days, it still applies.
